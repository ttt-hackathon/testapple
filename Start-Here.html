The situation

You work in purchasing at Laurel Ridge Bikeworks, an electric-bike maker in Hendersonville, NC. We build three models, about 120 bikes a week, from parts made by 13 suppliers in eight countries.

When a supplier has a problem, such as a storm, a fire, a strike or a backed-up port, we usually find out when a shipment doesn't arrive. By then the production line is days from stopping.

Your challenge

Build an early-warning tool that spots supply chain trouble before it reaches our production line, and raises an alert when it does.

Your team decides:

  • which warning signs matter for these suppliers
  • which sources give the earliest reliable warning
  • what counts as serious enough for an alert, and what the alert tells purchasing to do
  • what the tool looks like

Your tool is finished when it:

  1. Covers every supplier in data/suppliers.csv.
  2. Watches at least two kinds of real, current warning signs, each shown with its source link and date.
  3. Gives each supplier a risk level and says why.
  4. Has an alert: a rule for what counts as serious, plus a ready-to-send email to the purchasing team saying what happened and what to do.
  5. Works at your team's live link.

Finished early? Stretch goals

Pick any, in any order. Each one makes a good new section for the dashboard, built by whoever finishes first.

★ One star

  • Show your confidence. Next to each warning, show the source, its date, and how sure the tool is.
  • Map it. Plot every supplier on a map, colored by risk. How many of our eggs sit in one basket?

★★ Two stars

  • Find alternatives. For any supplier your tool flags, research 2 or 3 real alternative suppliers. Show where they are, what they make, and the trade-offs of switching.
  • Stock-up advisor. Find the parts that have only one source, take longest to replace and would stop the most bikes. Recommend which to stock up on, and by how many weeks.
  • What-if. Let the user "shut down" any supplier for a number of weeks. Show which bikes stop, when we run out of stock, and how much revenue is at risk.
  • Add a warning sign. Watch a kind of signal you didn't start with. Ask another team what they chose.

★★★ Three stars

  • Watch on its own. Type /loop 10m followed by instructions, and Claude re-checks your sources every 10 minutes, updates the dashboard, saves to GitHub and raises the alert when something new appears. Each check costs a little budget, so press Esc to stop it before you leave.
  • Real email alert. Have Claude write a Google Apps Script that checks your sources every few minutes and emails your team from your workshop Gmail when something serious appears. You'll paste it into script.google.com.
  • Red-team it. Have Claude write a misleading test warning about Cataloochee Cell Works, then check whether your tool overreacts or misses it. Use a test copy, never your live sources.

Laurel Ridge Bikeworks and Cataloochee Cell Works are fictional. The other suppliers are real companies at their real locations, but every relationship, price, order and inventory number in your folder is made up.
